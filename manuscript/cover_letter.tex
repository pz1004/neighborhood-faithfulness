\opening{Dear Professor Di Giovanni,}

Please consider the enclosed research article, ``\MsTitle'', for publication in the
\emph{Journal of Neuroscience Methods}.

Two-dimensional maps of trial-averaged responses, made with t-SNE or multidimensional scaling, are
read neighbor by neighbor throughout systems neuroscience, and analysts check them against the
averaged data or for stability across runs, resamples and halves of the trials. The manuscript
asks how often a neighborhood that passes such a check is faithful to the noise-free responses,
and answers with a method that estimates this from held-out repetitions.

The study reanalyzes two public datasets, THINGS-EEG2 and the Allen Brain Observatory two-photon
survey, and I am aware of the journal's policy for such work. I believe it meets that policy on
all three of its grounds:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Methodological innovation.} An estimator of the ``false comfort'' of any
  neighborhood check: held-out reference blocks, a simulation--extrapolation correction for the
  reference's own noise, subject-level bootstrap bounds, and validation in plasmode simulations.
  \item \textbf{Rigorous benchmarking.} A pre-specified comparison of six existing checks against the
  truth: per-point trustworthiness, scDEED, cross-validated distances, and recurrence across t-SNE
  seeds, bootstrap resamples and split halves. Four of the six left most false comfort in both
  datasets, the two strict resampling checks passed almost no neighborhoods, and none removed most
  false comfort.
  \item \textbf{Insight for experimental methodology.} At 8 trials, \EegFCeight\% of the
  neighborhoods that pass the usual check are not faithful in human EEG, and essentially all in
  mouse V1. An exploratory analysis relates the difference to the ratio of neighbor distance to
  trial noise, which bears directly on how many trials a study needs before its maps can be read
  locally.
\end{itemize}
Every claim, threshold and robustness variation was fixed in a protocol committed before the
analysis it governs was run, and the mouse data were selected by rule and analyzed only after
their protocol was fixed. In line with the journal's research data policy, the code, protocols and
derived results are deposited at \todo{repository and DOI} and cited in the article; the raw data
remain openly available from their providers.
