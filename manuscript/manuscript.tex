\section*{Abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Background:} Two-dimensional maps of trial-averaged responses, made with t-SNE or multidimensional scaling, are read neighbor by neighbor and checked against the averaged data (the plug-in check).
Because the map is fitted to the same noisy averages, such checks share its errors.

\noindent\textbf{New method:} We define the false comfort of a check as the share of the neighborhoods it passes that are not faithful to the noise-free responses, and estimate it from held-out repetitions, extrapolating away the reference's own noise.
A simulation study defines observable statuses that say when the estimate can be trusted, and tests whether its lower bound is calibrated.

\noindent\textbf{Results:} In human EEG (THINGS-EEG2; 10 participants, 200 images) with 8 trials per image, an estimated \EegFCeightSmaller\% of the t-SNE neighborhoods that passed the plug-in check were not faithful (calibrated lower bound \EegLeight\%), against \EegRandEight\% for a random selection.
At 16 and 32 trials the estimate was unresolved; disagreement with a 40-trial held-out reference fell from \EegDEight\% to \EegDThirtyTwo\%.
In mouse primary visual cortex (Allen Brain Observatory; 10 mice, 118 images), a 24-trial held-out reference confirmed under 0.1\% of the passing neighborhoods.

\noindent\textbf{Comparison with existing methods:} Of six common checks computed from the same trials, four selected faithful neighborhoods \BmEnrichLo--\BmEnrichHi{} times more often than a random selection in EEG, and two passed almost nothing.
Even the best-scoring 5\% of neighborhoods had an estimated false comfort of \BmFiveLo--\BmFiveHi\%.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusions:} Neighborhoods in maps of few-trial averages should be read against held-out repetitions deep enough for false comfort to be estimable.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Two-dimensional (2-D) maps of condition-averaged responses are a staple of systems neuroscience.
Representational similarity analysis (RSA) displays the responses to a set of stimuli with multidimensional scaling (MDS) \citep{kriegeskorte2008,kriegeskorte2008neuron,nili2014}, from fMRI to time-resolved MEG \citep{cichy2014}.
Object spaces are shown the same way \citep{hebart2023}, and nonlinear embeddings such as t-SNE \citep{vandermaaten2008} and UMAP are used to display population activity \citep{cunningham2014,gardner2022}.
Such maps are read locally: two stimuli that sit together are taken to evoke similar responses, and a stimulus's neighbors are taken to be the stimuli it is most similar to.

It is well documented that 2-D maps distort the neighborhoods of the data they are fitted to \citep{wattenberg2016,kobak2019,chari2023,cooley2019,lause2024}.
A family of quality criteria measures how well a map preserves those neighborhoods \citep{venna2001,kaski2003,chen2009,lee2009,venna2010}, and per-point tools flag the points whose neighborhoods are distorted: scDEED labels each point's embedding trustworthy or dubious against a permutation null \citep{xia2024}, EMBEDR compares each point with embeddings of randomized data \citep{johnson2022}, and DynamicViz measures each point's stability across bootstrap embeddings \citep{sun2023}; see also \citet{liu2025}.
All of these judge a map against the data it was made from, or against other maps of the same data.

In neuroscience those data are averages of a limited number of noisy trials.
The averages differ from the noise-free responses they estimate, and the map is fitted to the same averages that a check would use.
A check computed from the averages therefore shares its errors with the map, which is the conditional dependence that makes a diagnostic test look accurate when it is judged against a reference with correlated errors \citep{vacek1985}.
Related problems are known.
Hyperparameters chosen for a map's fidelity to noisy data overfit the noise, and maps are better judged against a denoised signal \citep{lin2024}.
Neighbor graphs computed from shallow data share few neighbors with graphs from deeper data \citep{ahlmann2023}.
RSA addresses trial noise with cross-validated distance estimates \citep{walther2016,guggenmos2018} and with noise ceilings \citep{nili2014,schutt2023}, but both concern the dissimilarity matrix as a whole, not the neighborhood of a single stimulus in a map.

We ask a narrow and practical question: how often is a displayed neighborhood that passes such a check faithful to the noise-free responses?
We call the share of passing neighborhoods that are not faithful the \emph{false comfort} of the check.
This paper makes five contributions.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item A per-neighborhood faithfulness label, the \emph{reach} of a displayed neighborhood, and the false comfort of any check that passes or fails neighborhoods (Section~\ref{sec:def}).
  \item An estimator of false comfort from held-out repetitions, with a simulation--extrapolation-type correction for the reference's own noise \citep{cook1994,devanarayan2002} and subject-level bootstrap bounds (Section~\ref{sec:est}).
  \item A simulation study of the estimator, planned with the ADEMP structure \citep{morris2019} and built on the real data \citep{vaughan2009,schreck2024}, that defines observable statuses saying when its estimate can be trusted and tests whether its bound is calibrated across simulated studies (Section~\ref{sec:val}).
  \item Measurements in human EEG and in two-photon imaging of mouse primary visual cortex (V1), with claims fixed before each analysis was run and reported according to their status (Sections~\ref{sec:data}--\ref{sec:claims}).
  \item A benchmark of six common checks against the truth and against a random selection of as many neighborhoods: per-point trustworthiness, scDEED's trustworthy label, cross-validated distances, and recurrence across t-SNE runs, bootstrap resamples and split halves of the trials (Section~\ref{sec:checks}).
\end{enumerate}
The estimator is not new in kind: it measures the classification accuracy of a decision made from noisy scores against the true scores, as in the classification-consistency literature of educational measurement \citep{livingston1995}.
What is new is its use to measure how faithful the neighborhoods of 2-D maps of trial-averaged responses are, the statuses that say where it can and cannot do so, and the measurements it yields.

\section{Materials and methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Neighborhood labels and false comfort}\label{sec:def}

Objects $i=1,\dots,n$ (stimuli) have noise-free mean responses $\mu_i\in\mathbb{R}^D$ in a representation fitted to separate trials of each random split (block $F$; Section~\ref{sec:rep}), so the truth is specific to that representation.
An analyst averages $R$ repetitions of each object into $c_i$, fits a 2-D map to $c_1,\dots,c_n$, and reads the set $E_i$ of the $k$ objects nearest to $i$ in the map.
For $j\in E_i$, let $\mathrm{rank}_i(j)\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}$ be the position of $j$ among the other objects ordered by their distance $\|\mu_i-\mu_\ell\|$ from $i$, with ties broken by object index.
The \emph{reach} of $i$ is the $m$-th smallest of these $k$ ranks, with $m=\lceil(1-\varepsilon)k\rceil$, and the displayed neighborhood is \emph{faithful at tolerance $k'$} if its reach is at most $k'$: at least $m$ of the $k$ displayed neighbors are among the $k'$ objects nearest to $i$ (Figure~\ref{fig:method}b).
Throughout, $k=10$, $\varepsilon=0.2$ and $k'=2k=20$, so at least 8 of a point's 10 displayed neighbors must be among its 20 nearest.

The \emph{naive label} $N_i$ is the same label computed with the averages $c_i$ in place of the $\mu_i$.
It is the check an analyst can run, and it is a thresholded, per-point form of the rank-based quality criteria of dimensionality reduction: with $\varepsilon=0$ and $k'=k$ it asks that the $k$ displayed neighbors be exactly the $k$ nearest objects, the per-point form of the criterion $Q_{NX}(k)$ \citep{lee2009}.
The \emph{truth label} $T_i$ uses the $\mu_i$.
For a given map we report the share of points called faithful, $\mathrm{NF}=\Pr(N=1)$; the share truly faithful, $\mathrm{TF}=\Pr(T=1)$; the mislabel rate, $q=\Pr(N\neq T)$; and the \emph{false comfort}
\begin{equation}
\FC=\Pr(T=0\mid N=1),\label{eq:fc}
\end{equation}
the share of the check's faithful calls that are wrong, one minus the check's precision.
The same definition applies to any check $X$ that passes or fails neighborhoods: $\FC_X=\Pr(T=0\mid X \text{ passes})$.
False comfort is undefined, not zero, for a map in which the check passes nothing.
Within a subject we pool counts over the maps of all random splits and seeds, so the quantity estimated is a protocol-level false comfort, the expected number of unfaithful passes divided by the expected number of passes.
In our data its median over subjects differed from the median of the per-map values by at most \PoolDiffMax{} at the deepest reference, for every check.

\subsection{Estimating false comfort from held-out repetitions}\label{sec:est}

The truth labels are unknown, but held-out repetitions estimate them (Figure~\ref{fig:method}a).
Each subject's repetitions of every image are dealt at random into three disjoint blocks, balanced over recording sessions (EEG) or over the early and late halves of an image's presentations (mouse V1).
Block $F$ fits the representation.
Block $U$ supplies the analyst's trials: the first $R$ of $U$ in an order that keeps every prefix balanced.
Block $H$ supplies the references.
$H$ is divided into disjoint, balanced sub-blocks of $R_h$ repetitions, and each sub-block gives a reference label $T^{(R_h)}_i$ for the analyst's displayed neighborhood $E_i$, computed like $N_i$ from the sub-block's means.
The reference's noise is independent of the analyst's, so $T^{(R_h)}$ approaches $T$ as $R_h$ grows, but at any finite $R_h$ it is itself noisy.
Reference labels always use plug-in distances between sub-block means; their bias, like their noise, shrinks as $R_h$ grows, and the extrapolation of Section~\ref{sec:extrap} targets the limit in which both vanish (Supplementary Section~S1.2).

\subsubsection{Disagreement and extrapolation}\label{sec:extrap}
For each subject, analyst budget $R$ and reference size $R_h$, the disagreement statistics are pooled over random splits, map seeds and sub-blocks:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item $d_{\FC}(R_h)$, the share of the analyst's faithful calls ($N_i=1$) whose reference label is not faithful ($T^{(R_h)}_i=0$), computed as a ratio of sums within the subject: the false comfort of Eq.~(\ref{eq:fc}) measured against the reference;
  \item $d_q(R_h)$, the share of points with $N_i\neq T^{(R_h)}_i$;
  \item $d_{\mathrm{TF}}(R_h)$, the share with $T^{(R_h)}_i=1$.
\end{itemize}
Each statistic is fitted as $a+b\,g(R_h)$ over the available reference sizes, and the intercept $a$ estimates its value for an infinitely large reference, that is, for the noise-free means.
We use two extrapolants, $g(R)=R^{-1/2}$ and $g(R)=R^{-1}$: the reference's noise has standard deviation proportional to $R_h^{-1/2}$ and variance proportional to $R_h^{-1}$, and near the limit the disagreement can follow either.
This is simulation--extrapolation (SIMEX) \citep{cook1994}, in the empirical form that uses replicate measurements \citep{devanarayan2002}, with the noise level varied by the size of the reference rather than by added noise.
Figure~\ref{fig:method}c shows the disagreement and both fits for every EEG subject; in mouse V1 the curves are flat at their ceiling (Figure~\ref{fig:method}d).

\subsubsection{Uncertainty and the lower bound}\label{sec:bounds}
The unit of inference is the subject.
Each extrapolant gives one estimate per subject, and we report the median over subjects.
Intervals come from 10{,}000 bootstrap resamples of subjects.
Each claim that a quantity exceeds a threshold is judged by the pre-specified lower bound $L$, the 2.5th percentile over resamples of the smaller of the two extrapolants' medians.
$L$ is meant as a one-sided 97.5\% bound for the median $\Theta$ of the subjects' false comfort in the population that the 10 subjects represent; for the 10 subjects' own median it is more conservative.
Whether it reaches that level was tested in simulated studies (Section~\ref{sec:sim}).
As a sensitivity bound we also report the second-smallest of the subjects' smaller estimates, $x_{(2)}$, which for exact subject values exceeds a population median with probability $11/1024\approx0.011$.
For claims that a quantity falls below a threshold, the upper bound $U$ is the 97.5th percentile of the larger one.

\subsubsection{Joint labels, for comparing checks}\label{sec:joint}
For a check $X$, the same machinery is applied to joint reference rates, pooled within each subject as before: $a_X(R_h)=\widehat{\Pr}(X\text{ passes},\,T^{(R_h)}=0)$, $b_X(R_h)=\widehat{\Pr}(N=1,\,X\text{ fails},\,T^{(R_h)}=0)$ and $c(R_h)=\widehat{\Pr}(N=1,\,T^{(R_h)}=0)$.
Each is extrapolated with both extrapolants, and then $\FC_X=\hat a_X/\Pr(X\text{ passes})$.
The share of the naive check's false-comfort neighborhoods that $X$ flags is $\mathrm{CATCH}_X=\hat b_X/\hat c$.
The share of all points whose neighborhood passes $X$ and is faithful is $\mathrm{YIELD}_X=\Pr(X\text{ passes})-\hat a_X$.
For $X=N$ this reproduces the estimator above exactly.
A random selection of as many neighborhoods as $X$ passes in each map, judged against the same references, gives the false comfort of a check without information, $\FC_{\mathrm{rand}}$, close to $1-\mathrm{TF}$.
The \emph{enrichment} $(1-\FC_X)/(1-\FC_{\mathrm{rand}})$, its median over subjects, is the factor by which the check's precision exceeds that of chance.

\subsection{Validating the estimator}\label{sec:val}

\subsubsection{The pre-specified plasmode check}\label{sec:plasmode}
A plasmode simulation \citep{vaughan2009,schreck2024} checks the estimator on real noise with a known truth.
For each subject, on one split, the truth is set to the means of block $H$ in the representation.
The analyst's trials and the reference sub-blocks are then drawn by resampling block $U$'s residuals within each image, rescaled by $\sqrt{n_U/(n_U-1)}$, and added to the truth.
Exact labels are computed against this truth, which is exact by construction, and the exact false comfort is compared with the estimate from the simulated references.
The confirmatory analyses used 20 draws per subject in the primary cell at 8 trials; the comparison of checks used 10 draws per subject, each with every check and all three map seeds.
The bias of the smaller estimate is the median over subjects of the smaller estimate minus the exact value.
A pre-specified guard lowered $L$ by this bias if it exceeded $+0.05$ (and raised $U$ by the corresponding amount if the larger estimate fell more than $0.05$ below the exact value).
The guard was computed from the primary t-SNE cell and applied to every claim, including those about cv-MDS maps.
This check covers one budget and one map; the simulation study below extends it.

\subsubsection{Simulation study}\label{sec:sim}
After the first results, a simulation study tested the estimator across regimes; its design, following the ADEMP structure of \citet{morris2019}, was fixed before it was run (Section~\ref{sec:prereg}; details in Supplementary Section~S1.6).
Every simulated dataset is built from one real subject's data in the measured features (EEG channel-time samples; mouse cells), so that it refits the representation on its own block $F$.
Its truth belongs to one of three families: T1, the plasmode truth above; T2, its projection onto the first five principal components of the image means, which is smoother and better separated; and T3, a mixture of the two whose gaps between each image's 8th and 20th nearest true distances match those estimated from the real data (in mouse V1 the match was T2 itself).
Each trial is the truth plus $\lambda$ times a residual resampled from block $U$, with $\lambda\in\{0.1,0.25,0.35,0.5,1,2\}$, and a noise-free control.
Two diagnostic mechanisms test known failure modes.
In the first, noise is shared: an offset common to every trial of an image within a session (EEG) or stratum (mouse V1) carries a share $\rho\in\{0.1,0.3\}$ of the noise variance.
In the second, a stress test, the analyst's trials are ten times less noisy than the reference's, so that most displayed neighborhoods are truly faithful.
The study has three layers.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Layer A} keeps one split's maps and labels fixed and regenerates only the references, 20 times, at the real reference sizes and at two and four times the largest.
  \item \textbf{Layer B} repeats the whole procedure 100 times per cell: for t-SNE maps under every truth and noise level at 8 trials, at the noise level of the data at 16 and 32 trials in EEG and 16 in mouse V1, and for cv-MDS maps at 8 trials.
  \item \textbf{Layer C} asks whether the bound $L$ is calibrated for the population median.
  Each of 1{,}000 virtual studies draws 10 subjects with replacement from 30 virtual subjects (the 10 real subjects at $\lambda\in\{0.8,1,1.25\}$), simulates a new dataset for each, and reproduces the published procedure exactly, including the subject bootstrap and, in the first 200 studies, the guard.
  Each virtual subject's true false comfort comes from 200 further datasets, and $\Theta$ is the median over the 30.
  Layer C ran under T1 and T3 for the claims that Layers A and B left standing (Section~\ref{sec:classes}; Supplementary Section~S1.8).
\end{itemize}
Performance was summarized by each extrapolant's median bias and root-mean-square error (RMSE), with Monte Carlo errors; by the share of repetitions whose exact value lay within the published range between the two extrapolants; and, in Layer C, by $\Pr(L>\Theta)$.
A development pilot on separate seeds fixed the noise grid, the estimator and the thresholds below before any validation seed was used; no candidate estimator outperformed the published pair, and no threshold set met the tuning rule, so the protocol's defaults were kept.

\subsubsection{Observable statuses}\label{sec:status}
Each real or simulated result receives one of four statuses, computed only from quantities an analyst can observe, per subject and then over subjects:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{too few passes}, if fewer than 5 subjects have at least 20 passing neighborhoods, pooled over maps;
  \item \textbf{saturated}, if the median disagreement at the deepest reference is at least 0.95 and its median fall across reference sizes at most 0.02;
  \item \textbf{unresolved}, if the line through the two shallower reference sizes predicts the deepest with a median error above 0.03 (a finite-depth check), or the two extrapolants differ by more than 0.10 in median;
  \item \textbf{estimable}, otherwise.
\end{itemize}
The mislabel rate gets its status from its own disagreement curve.
Supplementary Section~S1.7 gives the definitions in full.

\subsubsection{Reporting classes}\label{sec:classes}
Each real result is reported in one of three classes, by rules fixed before the validation was run.
Its \emph{matching} simulated cells are those of the same dataset and map type and the same status whose median deepest-reference disagreement and fall both lie within 0.05 of the real result's; the diagnostic mechanisms never match, and the worst matching cell decides.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Calibrated}: the status is estimable, the matching cells have $|$median bias$|\le0.03$ and RMSE $\le0.08$ for the smaller extrapolant, and Layer C gives $\Pr(L>\Theta)\le0.025$ plus two Monte Carlo errors under both truths.
  Only such a result may carry a threshold claim.
  \item \textbf{Model-dependent}: the status is estimable and the matching cells have $|$median bias$|\le0.05$ and RMSE $\le0.10$, but the result was not calibrated; or no simulated cell matches it (\emph{unvalidated}).
  It is reported as an estimate, without a threshold claim.
  \item \textbf{Finite-reference}: every other case.
  The result is reported as the disagreement with the deepest reference, with its subject-bootstrap interval, beside the same quantity for a random selection, and is never called false comfort.
\end{itemize}
The protocol also defined a strict reading, under which no result may carry a threshold claim because no single estimator was accurate in every regime (Section~\ref{sec:res-val}).
We report the cell-by-cell reading above; the strict reading changes only one result, which it makes model-dependent (Section~\ref{sec:res-status}).

\subsection{Data}\label{sec:data}

\subsubsection{Human EEG}\label{sec:data-eeg}
THINGS-EEG2 \citep{gifford2022,gifford2021data} recorded 64-channel EEG from 10 healthy adults (mean age 28.5 years, SD 4; 8 female, 2 male, as reported by the dataset's authors, who do not define the terms or say whether sex or gender was recorded) viewing images from the THINGS database \citep{hebart2019} in rapid serial visual presentation (100\,ms per image, stimulus onset asynchrony 200\,ms) with an orthogonal target-detection task.
We used the preprocessed test set: 200 object images, each presented 80 times over four sessions.
The dataset's authors epoched the data from $-0.2$ to $0.8$\,s, corrected the baseline, downsampled to 100\,Hz, kept 17 occipital and parietal channels, and applied multivariate noise normalization \citep{guggenmos2018} within each session, estimating the noise covariance from all of that session's trials.
That normalization therefore used trials that we later assign to every block; it is a covariance of residuals, pooled over images and applied identically to every block, so it is a dependence shared by the analyst's trials and the reference rather than a channel for image-specific signal.
We used the 80 post-stimulus samples (0--0.79\,s) of every channel, 1360 features per trial.
The published files store the 80 repetitions in a shuffled order; we recovered each repetition's session, and its rank within the session, by replaying the random permutation of the authors' preprocessing code (Supplementary Section~S1.1).
Blocks $F$, $U$ and $H$ held 8, 32 and 40 repetitions, drawn equally from the four sessions.

\subsubsection{Mouse V1}\label{sec:data-mouse}
From the Allen Brain Observatory Visual Coding two-photon survey \citep{devries2020,allen2016data}, a rule fixed before any recording was downloaded selected natural-scenes sessions (\texttt{three\_session\_B}) targeted to V1 in Cux2-CreERT2;Camk2a-tTA;Ai93(TITL-GCaMP6f) transgenic mice (\emph{Mus musculus}), which express GCaMP6f mainly in excitatory neurons of layers 2/3 and 4, at imaging depths of 175 or 275\,$\mu$m, one experiment per mouse (the 175\,$\mu$m one where a mouse was imaged at both).
Mice were taken in ascending donor identifier, and an experiment was included if it had at least 100 cells with a $\Delta F/F$ trace and exactly 50 presentations of each of the 118 images; inclusion stopped at 10 mice.
The first 10 candidates all qualified: \MouseCellsMin--\MouseCellsMax{} cells per experiment, \MouseMale{} male and \MouseFemale{} female mice aged \MouseAgeMin--\MouseAgeMax{} days at the recording session, eight imaged at 175\,$\mu$m and two at 275\,$\mu$m.
Sex is the sex recorded for each donor in the Allen Institute's metadata, and age is the time from the recorded date of birth to the session; both are listed per mouse in Supplementary Table~S1.
Each of the 118 grayscale natural scenes was shown for 0.25\,s without a gap, about 50 times in random order.
A trial's response was each cell's mean $\Delta F/F$ over the 0.5\,s after the presentation's onset.
Calcium signals are slow, so part of a response carries into the next presentation's window: consecutive 0.5\,s windows overlap by 7 of their 15 frames.
For a given pair of images, about \OvPerPair{} pairs of consecutive presentations fall one in block $U$ and one in block $H$, against $16\times24=384$ pairs of $U$ and $H$ trials; with random image order the overlap adds noise rather than image-specific bias, and the 0--0.25\,s window of the pre-specified variations does not overlap.
The 50 presentations of each image were ranked by time and split into an early and a late stratum of 25, and blocks $F$, $U$ and $H$ held 10, 16 and 24 repetitions, drawn equally from the two strata.

\subsubsection{Budgets and references}
Table~\ref{tab:design} summarizes the design.
The analyst's budgets were $R\in\{8,16,32\}$ in EEG and $R\in\{8,16\}$ in mouse V1, where 50 repetitions do not allow 32.
Reference sizes were $R_h\in\{8,20,40\}$ in EEG (5, 2 and 1 disjoint sub-blocks of $H$) and $R_h\in\{8,12,24\}$ in mouse V1 (3, 2 and 1).
An assessment therefore uses $F+R+H$ repetitions of each image: $48+R$ of 80 in EEG and $34+R$ of 50 in mouse V1.
Each subject contributed 10 random splits into blocks.
Every data file was checked against its recorded SHA-256 digest before it was read.

\subsection{Representation and maps}\label{sec:rep}

The representation was fitted on block $F$ only, and then applied to blocks $U$ and $H$ unchanged.
It projects the image means of $F$ onto their first 100 principal components, whitens that space with a Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage estimate \citep{ledoit2004} of $F$'s residual covariance (rescaled to its $n(r-1)$ degrees of freedom), and projects the whitened means onto their first $D=20$ principal components.
Euclidean distances in this space are therefore noise-normalized, as recommended for representational analyses \citep{walther2016,guggenmos2018}.

The primary map was t-SNE \citep{vandermaaten2008} of the analyst's averages, with perplexity 30, PCA initialization \citep{kobak2021} and the Barnes--Hut approximation of scikit-learn \citep{pedregosa2011}, repeated with three seeds.
The RSA-style map (``cv-MDS'') was metric MDS of $\sqrt{\max(0,\hat d^{\,2}_{ij})}$, where $\hat d^{\,2}_{ij}=\langle c^A_i-c^A_j,\;c^B_i-c^B_j\rangle$ are cross-validated squared distances between the means of two balanced halves $A$ and $B$ of the analyst's repetitions, unbiased for $\|\mu_i-\mu_j\|^2$ \citep{walther2016}.
Negative estimates therefore become zeros in the MDS input.
The naive labels of cv-MDS maps rank the signed estimates $\hat d^{\,2}_{ij}$, so they have no ties at zero; their reference labels use plug-in distances, as everywhere.
Each MDS map used one initialization per seed.

\subsection{Pre-specified claims and variations}\label{sec:claims}

The claims and decision rules were fixed before each analysis was run.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{C1.} In the primary analysis, false comfort at 8 trials exceeds 50\%; supported if $L>0.5$.
  \item \textbf{C1-RSA.} The same for cv-MDS maps.
  \item \textbf{C2} (EEG only). False comfort at 32 trials exceeds 25\%; supported if $L>0.25$.
  \item \textbf{C3.} The mislabel rate exceeds 10\% at every budget; supported if $L>0.10$ at each.
\end{itemize}
C1 was to count as general in a dataset if it held in at least 10 of 12 pre-specified variations.
Each variation changes one factor of the primary analysis: the cv-MDS pipeline; $k\in\{5,20\}$ (with $k'=2k$); $\varepsilon\in\{0.1,0.3\}$; t-SNE perplexity 15 or 50; PCA-2 maps; metric MDS of Euclidean distances; an alternative response window (0.05--0.50\,s in EEG, 0--0.25\,s in mouse V1); and an unwhitened representation (PCA to 20 dimensions of $F$'s means).
MDS maps used seeds of their own.
The bias guard of Section~\ref{sec:plasmode} applied to every claim.
Each claim's pre-specified verdict stands as recorded; the classes of Section~\ref{sec:classes} decide how it is reported.

\subsection{Common checks compared with the truth}\label{sec:checks}

The comparison asks whether any common check, computed from the analyst's own $R$ trials as an analyst would compute it, catches the false comfort.
It uses the primary maps of both datasets, re-created exactly with the confirmatory splits, seeds and code.
Each check passes or fails every displayed neighborhood.
\emph{$E_i$ recurs in a map $M$} means that the reach of $E_i$ computed from $M$'s 2-D distances is at most $k'$.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Plug-in (N).} The naive label: the reach of $E_i$ in the averages is at most $k'$.
  \item \textbf{Trustworthiness (TW).} Per-point trustworthiness
  \begin{equation}
  \tau_i=1-\frac{2}{k(2n-3k-1)}\sum_{j\in E_i}\max(0,\,r_{ij}-k),\label{eq:tw}
  \end{equation}
  with $r_{ij}$ the rank of $j$ among $i$'s neighbors in the averages; its mean over points is the usual trustworthiness score \citep{venna2001,kaski2003}.
  TW passes the points with the highest $\tau_i$ of Eq.~(\ref{eq:tw}), as many as N passes in the same map (ties included), so that the two are compared as rankings at the same number of neighborhoods read.
  \item \textbf{scDEED (SCD).} scDEED's ``trustworthy'' label \citep{xia2024}, at its default neighborhood of half the objects.
  The reliability score is the Pearson correlation between two vectors of 2-D distances from $i$: to its $n/2$ nearest objects in the averages, ordered by their distance there, and to its $n/2$ nearest objects in the map, ordered by 2-D distance.
  The null permutes every measured feature of the averages independently across objects, as scDEED permutes genes before its PCA step, passes the permuted averages through the same fixed representation, and maps them with the same t-SNE settings and seed; one permutation is drawn per map, and its $n$ scores form the null (Supplementary Section~S1.4).
  A point passes if its score reaches the null's 95th percentile.
  \item \textbf{Cross-validated distances (CV).} The reach of $E_i$ computed from the cross-validated squared distances of the RSA pipeline is at most $k'$.
  \item \textbf{Seed recurrence (SEED).} $E_i$ recurs in the maps of the same averages made with the other two t-SNE seeds.
  \item \textbf{Bootstrap recurrence (BOOT).} $E_i$ recurs in at least 95\% of 50 maps of bootstrap resamples, each drawing every object's $R$ trials with replacement, independently across objects.
  It is the neighborhood-level analogue of bootstrap stability tools such as DynamicViz \citep{sun2023}, with trials resampled instead of observations.
  \item \textbf{Split-half recurrence (SH).} $E_i$ recurs in the map of each half of the trials, using the halves of the RSA pipeline ($R/2$ trials each).
\end{itemize}
All maps in these checks used t-SNE with the display's settings and seed.
Each check is described by its false comfort at 8 trials and its enrichment over a random selection (Section~\ref{sec:joint}).
The checks with a continuous score (the naive reach for N, $\tau_i$, the scDEED score, the cross-validated reach and the bootstrap support) were also compared at matched acceptance, each passing the 5, 10, 20, 30 or 50\% of points with the best score in every map, ties at the cut-off included.
The protocol also pre-specified a classification of each check by its bounds: it \emph{leaves most false comfort} if $L_X>0.5$, \emph{removes most} if $U_X<0.5$, is otherwise undetermined, and is not evaluable where fewer than 5 subjects have a passing neighborhood.
Because those bounds were not calibrated for any check (Section~\ref{sec:classes}), the classification is reported only in the supplement and is not used for inference.
The share of the plug-in check's false-comfort neighborhoods that a check flags ($\mathrm{CATCH}_X$), its yield, its false comfort when used on top of the plug-in check, and all budgets above 8 are descriptive.

\subsection{Sensitivity analyses on the real data}\label{sec:sens}

Three descriptive analyses were specified together with the simulation study (Supplementary Section~S1.9).
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{EEG blocks from separate sessions.} Blocks $F$ and $U$ were each drawn from a single session, and block $H$ was the other two sessions (40 repetitions), for each of the 12 ordered pairs of sessions, at $R\in\{8,16\}$; the control drew balanced blocks of the same sizes.
  If noise shared within a session made the analyst's trials and the reference agree, separating them should raise the estimate.
  \item \textbf{A per-point rerun} re-created the primary and cv-MDS maps and every check with the committed splits and seeds, keeping each point's scores.
  It reproduced every committed count, and it gave the matched-acceptance comparison and the \emph{continuous overlap} of each displayed neighborhood with the deepest reference, $|E_i\cap N_{k'}|/k$, where $N_{k'}$ holds the reference's $k'$ objects nearest to $i$.
  \item \textbf{An alternative scDEED null} permuted the 20 coordinates of the representation instead of the measured features.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Exploratory analysis}\label{sec:explore}

To understand the difference between the datasets, we compared the distance from each image to its 8th nearest image with the noise of trial averages, in one split per subject.
Distances were cross-validated between two halves of the $U$ and $H$ repetitions (72 in EEG, 40 in mouse V1) in the 20-dimensional representation.
The noise $\sigma_{16}$ is the root-mean-square distance between two independent 16-trial means of the same image, computed from the pooled residual variance.
We also report the share of image pairs with a positive cross-validated distance and the split-half reliability of the distance matrix.
This analysis was not pre-specified.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Where the estimator can be trusted}\label{sec:res-val}

In the pre-specified plasmode check, the smaller estimate was nearly unbiased at 8 trials, with a median bias of \EegBias{} in EEG and \MouseBias{} in mouse V1, so the guard never applied (Supplementary Table~S3).
The simulation study shows that this held only in that regime (Table~\ref{tab:validation}, Figure~\ref{fig:validation}; every cell is listed in Supplementary Table~S4).
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{At 8 trials and the noise level of the EEG data} ($\lambda=1$), the smaller extrapolant's median bias was \ValBiasLo{} to \ValBiasHi{} under the three truths, with an RMSE of at most \ValRmseMax; the $1/R_h$ extrapolant overstated by \ValInvLo--\ValInvHi.
  \item \textbf{Elsewhere, neither extrapolant was right.}
  At moderate false comfort the smaller one underestimated by \ValModLo--\ValModHi, while $1/R_h$ stayed within \ValModInv{} of the exact value.
  At 16 trials $1/R_h$ overstated by \ValSixteenInvLo--\ValSixteenInvHi, and at 32 trials both overstated, by \ValThirtyTwoSmLo--\ValThirtyTwoSmHi{} and \ValThirtyTwoInvLo--\ValThirtyTwoInvHi.
  The range between the two extrapolants covered the exact value in \ValBracketLo--\ValBracketHi\% of repetitions in EEG, and in only \ValMouseBracketLo--\ValMouseBracketHi\% in mouse V1 at 16 trials, where both overstated by \ValMouseSixteenLo--\ValMouseSixteenHi.
  \item \textbf{The statuses separate the regimes.}
  Cells marked unresolved had an RMSE of \ValUnresRmseLo--\ValUnresRmseHi{} for the smaller extrapolant, against \ValEstRmseEeg{} pooled over the estimable EEG cells.
  Within the estimable cells accuracy still depended on the regime, which is why each real result is judged by its matching cells.
  Saturated cells were accurate (RMSE at most \ValSatRmse), and wherever the analyst's and the reference's trials were equally noisy, a saturated curve came with an exact false comfort of at least \ValSatExactMin.
  \item \textbf{A shallow, noisy reference against an accurate analyst} (the stress test) inflated the estimate by \ValStressLo{} to \ValStressHi, and every such cell was marked unresolved.
  References of two and four times the real depth (Layer A; Supplementary Table~S5) removed the inflation in EEG (from \LaEegToneGrid{} to \LaEegToneDeep) but not in mouse V1 (from \LaMouseToneGrid{} to \LaMouseToneDeep, and from \LaMouseTtwoGrid{} to \LaMouseTtwoDeep).
  \item \textbf{Noise shared by the analyst's trials and the reference} biased the estimate downward, by \ValDepEeg{} in EEG and \ValDepMouse{} in mouse V1 when it carried 30\% of the noise variance.
  Noise of this kind lowers the estimate, so it cannot explain a high one.
  \item \textbf{The mislabel rate} was recovered within $\pm$\ValQBias{} at the noise level of the data, and underestimated by up to \ValQMod{} at moderate false comfort.
\end{itemize}
With deeper references the disagreement kept falling beyond the real sizes: in EEG (T1, $\lambda=1$, 8 trials) from \LaCurveFirst{} with 8 reference trials to \LaCurveLast{} with 160, against an exact value of \LaExact, and the extrapolant fitted to the real sizes landed within \LaGrid{} of it (Figure~\ref{fig:validation}b).

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:disc}

A 2-D map of trial-averaged responses can agree with the averaged data, neighborhood by neighborhood, and still say little about the responses that the averaging estimates.
In human EEG at 8 trials, with a bound calibrated in simulated studies, most neighborhoods that passed the plug-in check were not faithful to the noise-free responses: an estimated \EegFCeightSmaller\%, against \EegRandEight\% for a random selection.
Beyond that regime, the estimator showed where it stops.
At 16 and 32 trials its extrapolation was unresolved, and in mouse V1 the held-out reference was saturated, so there only the disagreement with a finite reference can be reported; in mouse V1 that reference confirmed fewer than 0.1\% of the passing neighborhoods.

\subsection{Implications for practice}

The maps in these data are not meaningless.
In both datasets most image pairs had a positive cross-validated distance and the distance matrix replicated across independent trials (Table~\ref{tab:snr}), so the global arrangement of a map, which an RSA noise ceiling would assess, can be trusted more than its neighborhoods.
What fails is the local ordering: the distance from an image to its 8th nearest neighbor is comparable to, or smaller than, the noise between two 16-trial means.
Five practical consequences follow.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Neighbor-level readings of maps of few-trial averages (which stimulus sits next to which) should be treated as hypotheses, not findings, unless their false comfort has been estimated.
  \item Holding out repetitions has a cost.
  Our assessment used 48 repetitions of each image beside the analyst's $R$ in EEG and 34 in mouse V1, and even then the reference was saturated in mouse V1, and the extrapolation unresolved in EEG beyond 8 trials.
  \item Report the estimate with its status.
  Where the status is not estimable, report the disagreement with the reference beside that of a random selection, not an extrapolated value.
  \item Checks computed from the same trials carry information but are no substitute.
  In EEG they picked faithful neighborhoods \BmEnrichLo{} to \BmEnrichHi{} times more often than chance, yet even the 5\% of points with the best score had an estimated false comfort of \BmFiveLo--\BmFiveHi\%.
  A strict resampling check, such as recurrence in maps of both halves of the trials, passes almost nothing at small budgets, which is itself a useful warning.
  \item More trials help slowly.
  In EEG the disagreement with the 40-trial reference fell from \EegDEight\% at 8 trials to \EegDThirtyTwo\% at 32.
  Where the ratio of neighbor distance to trial noise is well below one, as in mouse V1, no budget in the range studied gave a reference that could confirm the maps' neighborhoods.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Comparison with existing methods}

\subsubsection{Quality criteria and per-point tools}
Rank-based quality criteria \citep{venna2001,kaski2003,chen2009,lee2009,venna2010} and per-point tools such as scDEED \citep{xia2024} and EMBEDR \citep{johnson2022} measure how well a map agrees with the data it was made from.
With trial-averaged data they measure agreement with the averages, and they inherit the averages' noise.
scDEED's permutation null, and EMBEDR's embeddings of randomized data, answer whether a neighborhood is better preserved than chance, which is a different question from whether it is faithful to the noise-free responses.
In EEG, per-point trustworthiness and scDEED's trustworthy label selected faithful neighborhoods \BmTWEnrich{} and \BmSCDEnrich{} times more often than a random selection, with estimated false comfort of \BmTWFC\% and \BmSCDFC\%; scDEED passed more neighborhoods than the plug-in check without being more often right, and how many it passed depended on its null.

\subsubsection{Stability}
Recurrence across t-SNE runs, bootstrap resamples (as in DynamicViz; \citealp{sun2023}) and split halves measures whether a neighborhood is stable, not whether it is faithful.
A neighborhood created by the noise in the averages recurs in every map of those averages, and a map's systematic distortions recur across seeds and resamples.
Seed recurrence passed a third of the EEG neighborhoods, with the lowest enrichment of the checks (\BmSEEDEnrich).
The strict resampling checks behaved differently: at 8 trials they passed almost nothing, and at 32 trials in EEG what they passed disagreed less often with the reference than what the plug-in check passed.

\subsubsection{Cross-validated distances and noise ceilings}
RSA's cross-validated distances remove the positive bias of plug-in distances \citep{walther2016,guggenmos2018}, and noise ceilings bound how well any model can match a dissimilarity matrix \citep{nili2014,schutt2023}.
Neither is designed to certify single neighborhoods.
Cross-validated distances made faithful calls rare, both as the map's distances (Section~\ref{sec:res-rsa}) and as a check on a t-SNE map, and they were among the most selective checks, but their estimated false comfort was still \BmCVFC\% as a check and \EegRsaFCeight\% as a map (model-dependent).

\subsubsection{What checks computed from the same trials can and cannot see}
Under Gaussian trial noise, the averages carry all the information that the trials hold about the noise-free means; the residuals add information only about the size of the noise (Supplementary Section~S1.3).
Split halves are the averages plus and minus a noise-only difference, bootstrap resamples are the averages plus resampled noise, and cross-validated squared distances are the plug-in ones minus a noise-only term.
Every check of Section~\ref{sec:checks} is therefore a function of the averages, the noise level and randomization.
Such a check can rank neighborhoods by how far their margins exceed the noise, which is why it beats a random selection, but it cannot measure its own false comfort: that needs data whose noise is independent of the averages, such as held-out repetitions.
We had predicted, in the comparison's protocol and from a stronger form of this argument, that all six checks would leave most false comfort; the enrichment over a random selection shows that the strong form was wrong.
Per-point certificates with family-wise error control are possible in principle, for instance through simultaneous inference on ranks \citep{mogstad2024}.
In our pilot on THINGS-EEG2, certificates built from simultaneous studentized bounds on pairwise distances resolved no neighborhood on the real data at 8 to 72 trials, nor in any of \CertDraws{} simulated draws with resampled real noise (Supplementary Section~S1.5).

\subsubsection{Denoised and deeper references}
\citet{lin2024} also judge maps against a denoised signal, obtained by PCA truncation of the same data, to choose hyperparameters for a whole map.
Here the reference comes from held-out repetitions, it is used at the level of individual neighborhoods, and false comfort is measured as a function of the number of trials.
Downsampling studies compare neighbor graphs from shallow data with graphs from deeper data \citep{ahlmann2023}, treating the deeper data as the truth; our extrapolation removes the reference's own noise instead, where its status allows.

\subsubsection{What the estimator adds}
The estimator needs held-out repetitions and gives a dataset-level answer: how much of what a check passes is wrong.
Its statuses say when that answer is available.
In our data it was available for EEG at 8 trials; at larger budgets and in mouse V1, only the disagreement with a finite reference could be reported.
The estimator does not certify individual neighborhoods, which the certificates of our pilot could not do at these signal-to-noise ratios either.
It tells an analyst whether reading a map's neighborhoods is warranted at all.

\subsection{Limitations}

Several limitations apply.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Scope of the data.} Both datasets are visual responses to static images.
  Other modalities (fMRI, spiking data), other stimulus sets and other response windows may have different ratios of neighbor distance to trial noise, and the effect may be weaker where that ratio is large.
  \item \textbf{One neighborhood definition.} The definition ($k=10$, $\varepsilon=0.2$, $k'=2k$) was varied one factor at a time, not jointly.
  We tested t-SNE, MDS and PCA maps, but not UMAP.
  \item \textbf{The estimator's validity rests on simulation.} Its accuracy and the calibration of its bound were established in simulations built from these data, under three families of truth, and a real result is trusted only as far as it resembles its matching simulated cells in observable quantities.
  The calibration in EEG relied on the estimator's slight downward bias, because the bootstrap rule alone is slightly liberal with 10 subjects, and the virtual population was built from the same 10 subjects.
  \item \textbf{The extrapolation assumes a functional form.} Neither extrapolant was right in every regime (Section~\ref{sec:res-val}), and the claims rest on the smaller one, which was accurate only in the regime of the EEG data at 8 trials.
  The plasmode truth is exact by construction; what it cannot show is how closely real data resemble it.
  \item \textbf{Shared noise makes the analyst and the reference agree.} Noise shared by the analyst's trials and the reference, such as session effects, slow drift, overlapping response windows or the per-session noise normalization of the EEG data, biases false comfort downward: in simulation by \ValDepEeg{} (EEG) and \ValDepMouse{} (mouse V1) when it carried 30\% of the noise variance, and separating the EEG sessions raised the estimate.
  Shared noise can therefore hide false comfort, but not produce it.
  \item \textbf{Pre-specification.} The EEG confirmation reused the pilot's data with new splits and seeds, and pre-specification was by commits to a version-controlled repository rather than by an external registry \citep{nosek2018}.
  The simulation study and the reporting classes were specified after the first results had been seen.
  The mouse data are the independent test of the pre-specified claims.
  \item \textbf{Representation.} The representation was noise-normalized.
  Without whitening, the references were saturated in both datasets.
  \item \textbf{Sex and gender.} The EEG participants were reported as 8 female and 2 male, and the mice were \MouseMale{} male and \MouseFemale{} female (Sections~\ref{sec:data-eeg} and~\ref{sec:data-mouse}).
  With 10 subjects per dataset, sex and gender were not analyzed, and neither the human nor the mouse sample can show whether they affect the ratio of neighbor distance to trial noise.
  In mouse V1, the disagreement with the 24-trial reference was at least \MouseDminSubject\% in every mouse of either sex (Supplementary Table~S1).
  The EEG sample, ten healthy young adults, is also small and homogeneous in age, which limits how far the human estimates generalize.
  \item \textbf{The comparison of checks} used the primary analysis of each dataset only.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Conclusions}

At the trial budgets common in systems neuroscience, neighborhoods that look faithful in 2-D maps of trial-averaged responses are often not faithful to the responses the averaging estimates: in human EEG at 8 trials, an estimated four in five were not, with a calibrated bound.
Checks computed from the same trials help, but they do not repair this.
Whether false comfort can be estimated at all depends on the depth of the held-out reference, and the estimator's statuses say when it can; where it cannot, the disagreement with the reference is what should be reported.
Local neighborhoods in such maps should therefore be read against held-out repetitions.
The estimator and its statuses are released with the paper, together with the code, protocols and results.
