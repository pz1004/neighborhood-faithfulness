\section*{Abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Background:} Two-dimensional maps of trial-averaged responses, made with t-SNE or multidimensional scaling, are read neighbor by neighbor, and analysts check them against the averaged data (the plug-in check).
Because the map is fitted to the same noisy averages, such checks share its errors.

\noindent\textbf{New method:} We define the false comfort of a check as the share of the neighborhoods it passes that are not faithful to the noise-free responses.
We estimate it from held-out repetitions, with a simulation--extrapolation correction for the reference's noise and subject-bootstrap bounds, and validate the estimator in plasmode simulations.

\noindent\textbf{Results:} Pre-specified analyses used human EEG (THINGS-EEG2; 10 participants, 200 images) and two-photon imaging of mouse primary visual cortex (V1; Allen Brain Observatory; 10 mice, 118 images).
At 8 trials, \EegFCeight\% of the neighborhoods that passed the plug-in check were not faithful in EEG (conservative bound \EegLeight\%), and essentially all were in mouse V1 (bound \MouseLeight\%).
Maps of cross-validated distances passed fewer neighborhoods, most of them unfaithful.
The result held in \EegCells{} and \MouseCells{} of 12 pre-specified variations.

\noindent\textbf{Comparison with existing methods:} Of six common checks computed from the same trials, per-point trustworthiness, scDEED, cross-validated distances and recurrence across t-SNE seeds left most false comfort in both datasets.
Bootstrap and split-half recurrence passed almost no neighborhoods at 8 trials.
None removed most false comfort.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusions:} Checks computed from the same trials cannot reveal where trial averages are wrong.
Local neighborhoods of such maps should be read against held-out repetitions, which the estimator makes practical.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Two-dimensional (2-D) maps of condition-averaged responses are a staple of systems neuroscience.
Representational similarity analysis (RSA) displays the responses to a set of stimuli with multidimensional scaling (MDS) \citep{kriegeskorte2008,kriegeskorte2008neuron,nili2014}, from fMRI to time-resolved MEG \citep{cichy2014}.
Object spaces are shown the same way \citep{hebart2023}, and nonlinear embeddings such as t-SNE \citep{vandermaaten2008} and UMAP are used to display population activity \citep{cunningham2014,gardner2022}.
Such maps are read locally: two stimuli that sit together are taken to evoke similar responses, and a stimulus's neighbors are taken to be the stimuli it is most similar to.

It is well documented that 2-D maps distort the neighborhoods of the data they are fitted to \citep{wattenberg2016,kobak2019,chari2023,cooley2019,lause2024}.
A family of quality criteria measures how well a map preserves those neighborhoods \citep{venna2001,kaski2003,chen2009,lee2009,venna2010}, and per-point tools flag the points whose neighborhoods are distorted: scDEED labels each point's embedding trustworthy or dubious against a permutation null \citep{xia2024}, EMBEDR compares each point with embeddings of randomized data \citep{johnson2022}, and DynamicViz measures each point's stability across bootstrap embeddings \citep{sun2023}; see also \citet{liu2025}.
All of these judge a map against the data it was made from, or against other maps of the same data.

In neuroscience those data are averages of a limited number of noisy trials.
The averages differ from the noise-free responses they estimate, and the map is fitted to the same averages that a check would use.
A check computed from the averages therefore shares its errors with the map, which is the conditional dependence that makes a diagnostic test look accurate when it is judged against a reference with correlated errors \citep{vacek1985}.
Related problems are known.
Hyperparameters chosen for a map's fidelity to noisy data overfit the noise, and maps are better judged against a denoised signal \citep{lin2024}.
Neighbor graphs computed from shallow data share few neighbors with graphs from deeper data \citep{ahlmann2023}.
RSA addresses trial noise with cross-validated distance estimates \citep{walther2016,guggenmos2018} and with noise ceilings \citep{nili2014,schutt2023}, but both concern the dissimilarity matrix as a whole, not the neighborhood of a single stimulus in a map.

We ask a narrow and practical question: how often is a displayed neighborhood that passes such a check faithful to the noise-free responses?
We call the share of passing neighborhoods that are not faithful the \emph{false comfort} of the check.
This paper makes four contributions.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item A per-neighborhood faithfulness label, the \emph{reach} of a displayed neighborhood, and the false comfort of any check that passes or fails neighborhoods (Section~\ref{sec:def}).
  \item An estimator of false comfort from held-out repetitions, with a simulation--extrapolation-type correction for the reference's own noise \citep{cook1994,devanarayan2002} and subject-level bootstrap bounds, validated in plasmode simulations \citep{vaughan2009,schreck2024} (Section~\ref{sec:est}).
  \item Pre-specified measurements in human EEG and in two-photon imaging of mouse primary visual cortex (V1), with claims, thresholds and robustness variations fixed before each analysis was run (Sections~\ref{sec:data}--\ref{sec:claims}).
  \item A pre-specified benchmark of six common checks against the truth: per-point trustworthiness, scDEED's trustworthy label, cross-validated distances, and recurrence across t-SNE runs, bootstrap resamples and split halves of the trials (Section~\ref{sec:checks}).
\end{enumerate}
The estimator is not new in kind: it measures the classification accuracy of a decision made from noisy scores against the true scores, as in the classification-consistency literature of educational measurement \citep{livingston1995}.
What is new is its use to measure how faithful the neighborhoods of 2-D maps of trial-averaged responses are, and the measurements it yields.

\section{Materials and methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Neighborhood labels and false comfort}\label{sec:def}

Objects $i=1,\dots,n$ (stimuli) have noise-free mean responses $\mu_i\in\mathbb{R}^D$ in a fixed representation (Section~\ref{sec:rep}).
An analyst averages $R$ repetitions of each object into $c_i$, fits a 2-D map to $c_1,\dots,c_n$, and reads the set $E_i$ of the $k$ objects nearest to $i$ in the map.
For $j\in E_i$, let $\mathrm{rank}_i(j)\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}$ be the position of $j$ among the other objects ordered by their distance $\|\mu_i-\mu_\ell\|$ from $i$, with ties broken by object index.
The \emph{reach} of $i$ is the $m$-th smallest of these $k$ ranks, with $m=\lceil(1-\varepsilon)k\rceil$, and the displayed neighborhood is \emph{faithful at tolerance $k'$} if its reach is at most $k'$: at least $m$ of the $k$ displayed neighbors are among the $k'$ objects nearest to $i$ (Figure~\ref{fig:method}b).
Throughout, $k=10$, $\varepsilon=0.2$ and $k'=2k=20$, so at least 8 of a point's 10 displayed neighbors must be among its 20 nearest.

The \emph{naive label} $N_i$ is the same label computed with the averages $c_i$ in place of the $\mu_i$.
It is the check an analyst can run, and it is a thresholded, per-point form of the rank-based quality criteria of dimensionality reduction: with $\varepsilon=0$ and $k'=k$ it asks that the $k$ displayed neighbors be exactly the $k$ nearest objects, the per-point form of the criterion $Q_{NX}(k)$ \citep{lee2009}.
The \emph{truth label} $T_i$ uses the $\mu_i$.
For a given map we report the share of points called faithful, $\mathrm{NF}=\Pr(N=1)$; the share truly faithful, $\mathrm{TF}=\Pr(T=1)$; the mislabel rate, $q=\Pr(N\neq T)$; and the \emph{false comfort}
\begin{equation}
\FC=\Pr(T=0\mid N=1),\label{eq:fc}
\end{equation}
the share of the check's faithful calls that are wrong, one minus the check's precision.
The same definition applies to any check $X$ that passes or fails neighborhoods: $\FC_X=\Pr(T=0\mid X \text{ passes})$.

\subsection{Estimating false comfort from held-out repetitions}\label{sec:est}

The truth labels are unknown, but held-out repetitions estimate them (Figure~\ref{fig:method}a).
Each subject's repetitions of every image are dealt at random into three disjoint blocks, balanced over recording sessions (EEG) or over the early and late halves of an image's presentations (mouse V1).
Block $F$ fits the representation.
Block $U$ supplies the analyst's trials: the first $R$ of $U$ in an order that keeps every prefix balanced.
Block $H$ supplies the references.
$H$ is divided into disjoint, balanced sub-blocks of $R_h$ repetitions, and each sub-block gives a reference label $T^{(R_h)}_i$ for the analyst's displayed neighborhood $E_i$, computed like $N_i$ from the sub-block's means.
The reference's noise is independent of the analyst's, so $T^{(R_h)}$ approaches $T$ as $R_h$ grows, but at any finite $R_h$ it is itself noisy.
Reference labels always use plug-in distances between sub-block means; their bias, like their noise, shrinks as $R_h$ grows and is removed by the extrapolation of Section~\ref{sec:extrap} (Supplementary Section~S1.2).

\subsubsection{Disagreement and extrapolation}\label{sec:extrap}
For each subject, analyst budget $R$ and reference size $R_h$, the disagreement statistics are pooled over random splits, map seeds and sub-blocks:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item $d_{\FC}(R_h)$, the share of the analyst's faithful calls ($N_i=1$) whose reference label is not faithful ($T^{(R_h)}_i=0$), computed as a ratio of sums within the subject: the false comfort of Eq.~(\ref{eq:fc}) measured against the reference;
  \item $d_q(R_h)$, the share of points with $N_i\neq T^{(R_h)}_i$;
  \item $d_{\mathrm{TF}}(R_h)$, the share with $T^{(R_h)}_i=1$.
\end{itemize}
Each statistic is fitted as $a+b\,g(R_h)$ over the available reference sizes, and the intercept $a$ estimates its value for an infinitely large reference, that is, for the noise-free means.
We use two extrapolants, $g(R)=R^{-1/2}$ and $g(R)=R^{-1}$: the reference's noise has standard deviation proportional to $R_h^{-1/2}$ and variance proportional to $R_h^{-1}$, and near the limit the disagreement can follow either.
This is simulation--extrapolation (SIMEX) \citep{cook1994}, in the empirical form that uses replicate measurements \citep{devanarayan2002}, with the noise level varied by the size of the reference rather than by added noise.
Figure~\ref{fig:method}c,d shows the disagreement and both fits for every subject.

\subsubsection{Uncertainty and the conservative bound}\label{sec:bounds}
The unit of inference is the subject.
Each extrapolant gives one estimate per subject, and we report the median over subjects.
Intervals come from 10{,}000 bootstrap resamples of subjects.
Each claim that a quantity exceeds a threshold is judged by a conservative lower bound $L$, the 2.5th percentile over resamples of the smaller of the two extrapolants' medians.
For claims that a quantity falls below a threshold, the upper bound $U$ is the 97.5th percentile of the larger one.

\subsubsection{Joint labels, for comparing checks}\label{sec:joint}
For a check $X$, the same machinery is applied to joint reference rates, pooled within each subject as before: $a_X(R_h)=\widehat{\Pr}(X\text{ passes},\,T^{(R_h)}=0)$, $b_X(R_h)=\widehat{\Pr}(N=1,\,X\text{ fails},\,T^{(R_h)}=0)$ and $c(R_h)=\widehat{\Pr}(N=1,\,T^{(R_h)}=0)$.
Each is extrapolated with both extrapolants, and then $\FC_X=\hat a_X/\Pr(X\text{ passes})$.
The share of the naive check's false-comfort neighborhoods that $X$ flags is $\mathrm{CATCH}_X=\hat b_X/\hat c$.
The share of all points whose neighborhood passes $X$ and is faithful is $\mathrm{YIELD}_X=\Pr(X\text{ passes})-\hat a_X$.
For $X=N$ this reproduces the estimator above exactly.

\subsection{Semi-synthetic validation of the estimator}\label{sec:plasmode}

A plasmode simulation \citep{vaughan2009,schreck2024} checks the estimator on real noise with a known truth.
For each subject, on one split, the truth is set to the means of block $H$ in the representation.
The analyst's trials and the reference sub-blocks are then drawn by resampling block $U$'s residuals within each image, rescaled by $\sqrt{n_U/(n_U-1)}$, and added to the truth.
Exact labels are computed against the known truth, and the exact false comfort is compared with the estimate from the simulated references.
The confirmatory analyses used 20 draws per subject in the primary cell; the comparison of checks used 10 draws per subject, each with every check and all three map seeds.
The bias of the conservative estimator is the median over subjects of the smaller estimate minus the exact value.
A pre-specified guard lowered $L$ by this bias if it exceeded $+0.05$ (and raised $U$ by the corresponding amount if the larger estimate fell more than $0.05$ below the exact value).

\subsection{Data}\label{sec:data}

\subsubsection{Human EEG}\label{sec:data-eeg}
THINGS-EEG2 \citep{gifford2022,gifford2021data} recorded 64-channel EEG from 10 healthy adults (mean age 28.5 years, SD 4; 8 female, 2 male, as reported by the dataset's authors, who do not define the terms or say whether sex or gender was recorded) viewing images from the THINGS database \citep{hebart2019} in rapid serial visual presentation (100\,ms per image, stimulus onset asynchrony 200\,ms) with an orthogonal target-detection task.
We used the preprocessed test set: 200 object images, each presented 80 times over four sessions.
The dataset's authors epoched the data from $-0.2$ to $0.8$\,s, corrected the baseline, downsampled to 100\,Hz, kept 17 occipital and parietal channels, and applied multivariate noise normalization \citep{guggenmos2018} within each session.
We used the 80 post-stimulus samples (0--0.79\,s) of every channel, 1360 features per trial.
The published files store the 80 repetitions in a shuffled order; we recovered each repetition's session by replaying the random permutation of the authors' preprocessing code (Supplementary Section~S1.1).
Blocks $F$, $U$ and $H$ held 8, 32 and 40 repetitions, drawn equally from the four sessions.

\subsubsection{Mouse V1}\label{sec:data-mouse}
From the Allen Brain Observatory Visual Coding two-photon survey \citep{devries2020,allen2016data}, a rule fixed before any recording was downloaded selected natural-scenes sessions (\texttt{three\_session\_B}) targeted to V1 in Cux2-CreERT2;Camk2a-tTA;Ai93(TITL-GCaMP6f) transgenic mice (\emph{Mus musculus}), which express GCaMP6f mainly in excitatory neurons of layers 2/3 and 4, at imaging depths of 175 or 275\,$\mu$m, one experiment per mouse (the 175\,$\mu$m one where a mouse was imaged at both).
Mice were taken in ascending donor identifier, and an experiment was included if it had at least 100 cells with a $\Delta F/F$ trace and exactly 50 presentations of each of the 118 images; inclusion stopped at 10 mice.
The first 10 candidates all qualified: \MouseCellsMin--\MouseCellsMax{} cells per experiment, \MouseMale{} male and \MouseFemale{} female mice aged \MouseAgeMin--\MouseAgeMax{} days at the recording session, eight imaged at 175\,$\mu$m and two at 275\,$\mu$m.
Sex is the sex recorded for each donor in the Allen Institute's metadata, and age is the time from the recorded date of birth to the session; both are listed per mouse in Supplementary Table~S1.
Each of the 118 grayscale natural scenes was shown for 0.25\,s without a gap, about 50 times in random order.
A trial's response was each cell's mean $\Delta F/F$ over the 0.5\,s after the presentation's onset.
Calcium signals are slow, so part of a response carries into the next presentation's window; with random image order this adds noise rather than image-specific bias.
The 50 presentations of each image were ranked by time and split into an early and a late stratum of 25, and blocks $F$, $U$ and $H$ held 10, 16 and 24 repetitions, drawn equally from the two strata.

\subsection{Representation and maps}\label{sec:rep}

The representation was fitted on block $F$ only, and then applied to blocks $U$ and $H$ unchanged.
It projects the image means of $F$ onto their first 100 principal components, whitens that space with a Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage estimate \citep{ledoit2004} of $F$'s residual covariance (rescaled to its $n(r-1)$ degrees of freedom), and projects the whitened means onto their first $D=20$ principal components.
Euclidean distances in this space are therefore noise-normalized, as recommended for representational analyses \citep{walther2016,guggenmos2018}.

The primary map was t-SNE \citep{vandermaaten2008} of the analyst's averages, with perplexity 30, PCA initialization \citep{kobak2021} and the Barnes--Hut approximation of scikit-learn \citep{pedregosa2011}, repeated with three seeds.
The RSA-style map (``cv-MDS'') was metric MDS of $\sqrt{\max(0,\hat d^{\,2}_{ij})}$, where $\hat d^{\,2}_{ij}=\langle c^A_i-c^A_j,\;c^B_i-c^B_j\rangle$ are cross-validated squared distances between the means of two balanced halves $A$ and $B$ of the analyst's repetitions, unbiased for $\|\mu_i-\mu_j\|^2$ \citep{walther2016}; its naive labels used the same cross-validated distances.
Each MDS map used one initialization per seed.

\subsection{Pre-specified claims and variations}\label{sec:claims}

The claims and decision rules were fixed before each analysis was run.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{C1.} In the primary analysis, false comfort at 8 trials exceeds 50\%; supported if $L>0.5$.
  \item \textbf{C1-RSA.} The same for cv-MDS maps.
  \item \textbf{C2} (EEG only). False comfort at 32 trials exceeds 25\%; supported if $L>0.25$.
  \item \textbf{C3.} The mislabel rate exceeds 10\% at every budget; supported if $L>0.10$ at each.
\end{itemize}
C1 was to count as general in a dataset if it held in at least 10 of 12 pre-specified variations.
Each variation changes one factor of the primary analysis: the cv-MDS pipeline; $k\in\{5,20\}$ (with $k'=2k$); $\varepsilon\in\{0.1,0.3\}$; t-SNE perplexity 15 or 50; PCA-2 maps; metric MDS of Euclidean distances; an alternative response window (0.05--0.50\,s in EEG, 0--0.25\,s in mouse V1); and an unwhitened representation (PCA to 20 dimensions of $F$'s means).
MDS maps used seeds of their own.
A bias guard (Section~\ref{sec:plasmode}) applied to every claim.

\subsection{Common checks compared with the truth}\label{sec:checks}

The comparison asks whether any common check, computed from the analyst's own $R$ trials as an analyst would compute it, catches the false comfort.
It uses the primary maps of both datasets, re-created exactly with the confirmatory splits, seeds and code.
Each check passes or fails every displayed neighborhood.
\emph{$E_i$ recurs in a map $M$} means that the reach of $E_i$ computed from $M$'s 2-D distances is at most $k'$.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Plug-in (N).} The naive label: the reach of $E_i$ in the averages is at most $k'$.
  \item \textbf{Trustworthiness (TW).} Per-point trustworthiness
  \begin{equation}
  \tau_i=1-\frac{2}{k(2n-3k-1)}\sum_{j\in E_i}\max(0,\,r_{ij}-k),\label{eq:tw}
  \end{equation}
  with $r_{ij}$ the rank of $j$ among $i$'s neighbors in the averages; its mean over points is the usual trustworthiness score \citep{venna2001,kaski2003}.
  TW passes the points with the highest $\tau_i$ of Eq.~(\ref{eq:tw}), as many as N passes in the same map (ties included), so that the two are compared as rankings at the same number of neighborhoods read.
  \item \textbf{scDEED (SCD).} scDEED's ``trustworthy'' label \citep{xia2024}, at its default neighborhood of half the objects.
  The reliability score is the Pearson correlation between two vectors of 2-D distances from $i$: to its $n/2$ nearest objects in the averages, ordered by their distance there, and to its $n/2$ nearest objects in the map, ordered by 2-D distance.
  The null permutes every measured feature of the averages independently across objects, as scDEED permutes genes before its PCA step, passes the permuted averages through the same fixed representation, and maps them with the same t-SNE settings and seed (Supplementary Section~S1.4).
  A point passes if its score reaches the null's 95th percentile.
  \item \textbf{Cross-validated distances (CV).} The reach of $E_i$ computed from the cross-validated squared distances of the RSA pipeline is at most $k'$.
  \item \textbf{Seed recurrence (SEED).} $E_i$ recurs in the maps of the same averages made with the other two t-SNE seeds.
  \item \textbf{Bootstrap recurrence (BOOT).} $E_i$ recurs in at least 95\% of 50 maps of bootstrap resamples, each drawing every object's $R$ trials with replacement, independently across objects.
  It is the neighborhood-level analogue of bootstrap stability tools such as DynamicViz \citep{sun2023}, with trials resampled instead of observations.
  \item \textbf{Split-half recurrence (SH).} $E_i$ recurs in the map of each half of the trials, using the halves of the RSA pipeline ($R/2$ trials each).
\end{itemize}
All maps in these checks used t-SNE with the display's settings and seed.
The primary quantity is each check's false comfort at 8 trials, $\FC_X$.
The pre-specified classification, in each dataset, is: the check \emph{leaves most false comfort} if $L_X>0.5$; it \emph{removes most false comfort} if $U_X<0.5$; otherwise the result is undetermined.
A check was to be declared not evaluable where fewer than 5 subjects had any passing neighborhood.
The share of the plug-in check's false-comfort neighborhoods that a check flags ($\mathrm{CATCH}_X$), its yield of truly faithful neighborhoods ($\mathrm{YIELD}_X$), its false comfort when used on top of the plug-in check, and all budgets above 8 are descriptive.

\subsection{Exploratory analysis}\label{sec:explore}

To understand the difference between the datasets, we compared the distance from each image to its 8th nearest image with the noise of trial averages, in one split per subject.
Distances were cross-validated between two halves of the $U$ and $H$ repetitions (72 in EEG, 40 in mouse V1) in the 20-dimensional representation.
The noise $\sigma_{16}$ is the root-mean-square distance between two independent 16-trial means of the same image, computed from the pooled residual variance.
We also report the share of image pairs with a positive cross-validated distance and the split-half reliability of the distance matrix.
This analysis was not pre-specified.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:disc}

A 2-D map of trial-averaged responses can agree with the averaged data, neighborhood by neighborhood, and still say little about the responses that the averaging estimates.
In two independent datasets, with claims fixed in advance, most neighborhoods that passed the plug-in check at 8 trials were not faithful to the noise-free responses: \EegFCeight\% in human EEG and essentially all in mouse V1.
Cross-validated distances made the check stricter but not accurate, and the result held across the pre-specified variations of the neighborhood definition, the map and the representation.

\subsection{Implications for practice}

The maps in these data are not meaningless.
In both datasets most image pairs were reliably distinguishable and the distance matrix replicated across independent trials (Table~\ref{tab:snr}), so the global arrangement of a map, which an RSA noise ceiling would certify, can be trusted more than its neighborhoods.
What fails is the local ordering: the distance from an image to its 8th nearest neighbor is comparable to, or smaller than, the noise between two 16-trial means.
Five practical consequences follow.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Neighbor-level readings of maps of few-trial averages (which stimulus sits next to which) should be treated as hypotheses, not findings, unless their false comfort has been estimated.
  \item When the design has repetitions to spare, hold some out.
  With the estimator of Section~\ref{sec:est}, a held-out block at two or more sub-block sizes gives the false comfort of the analyst's map and of any check the analyst uses, with a conservative bound.
  \item Report that estimate with the map, as one reports a noise ceiling with a model comparison.
  \item Checks computed from the same trials are no substitute.
  Scores against the averages and consistency across t-SNE runs pass many unfaithful neighborhoods.
  A strict resampling check, such as recurrence in maps of both halves of the trials, is the safer of these: at small budgets it passes almost nothing, and what it passes is more often faithful than what the permissive checks pass.
  \item More trials help slowly.
  In EEG, false comfort fell from \EegFCeight\% at 8 trials to \EegFCthirtytwo\% at 32.
  Where the ratio of neighbor distance to trial noise is well below one, as in mouse V1, no budget in the range studied gives faithful neighborhoods.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Comparison with existing methods}

\subsubsection{Quality criteria and per-point tools}
Rank-based quality criteria \citep{venna2001,kaski2003,chen2009,lee2009,venna2010} and per-point tools such as scDEED \citep{xia2024} and EMBEDR \citep{johnson2022} measure how well a map agrees with the data it was made from.
With trial-averaged data they measure agreement with the averages, and they inherit the averages' noise.
scDEED's permutation null, and EMBEDR's embeddings of randomized data, answer whether a neighborhood is better preserved than chance, which is a different question from whether it is faithful to the noise-free responses.
In both datasets, per-point trustworthiness and scDEED's trustworthy label left most false comfort, and scDEED passed more EEG neighborhoods than the plug-in check without being more often right (Table~\ref{tab:checks}).

\subsubsection{Stability}
Recurrence across t-SNE runs, bootstrap resamples (as in DynamicViz; \citealp{sun2023}) and split halves measures whether a neighborhood is stable, not whether it is faithful.
A neighborhood created by the noise in the averages recurs in every map of those averages, and a map's systematic distortions recur across seeds and resamples.
Seed recurrence passed a third of the EEG neighborhoods, most of them not faithful.
The strict resampling checks behaved differently: at 8 trials they passed almost nothing, which is itself a useful warning, and at 32 trials in EEG what they passed was more often faithful.
Among the checks we tested, only a strict resampling check avoided passing many unfaithful neighborhoods, but at 8 trials even split-half recurrence was wrong about roughly half of the little it passed.

\subsubsection{Cross-validated distances and noise ceilings}
RSA's cross-validated distances remove the positive bias of plug-in distances \citep{walther2016,guggenmos2018}, and noise ceilings bound how well any model can match a dissimilarity matrix \citep{nili2014,schutt2023}.
Neither is designed to certify single neighborhoods.
Cross-validated distances made faithful calls rare, both as the map's distances (Section~\ref{sec:res-rsa}) and as a check on a t-SNE map, but the calls that remained were still mostly wrong.

\subsubsection{Why checks computed from the same trials cannot see the error}
Under Gaussian trial noise, the averages carry all the information that the trials hold about the noise-free means; the residuals add information only about the size of the noise (Supplementary Section~S1.3).
Split halves are the averages plus and minus a noise-only difference, bootstrap resamples are the averages plus resampled noise, and cross-validated squared distances are the plug-in ones minus a noise-only term.
Every check of Section~\ref{sec:checks} is therefore a function of the averages, the noise level and randomization.
It can be stricter than the plug-in check, but it cannot tell where the averages themselves are wrong.
We recorded this argument, and the prediction that all six checks would leave most false comfort, in the comparison's protocol before it was run.
The prediction held for the four checks that passed many neighborhoods.
The two strict checks did better only by passing almost nothing: strictness selects the neighborhoods whose margins are large relative to the noise, and at 8 trials almost none exist.
Per-point certificates with family-wise error control are possible in principle, for instance through simultaneous inference on ranks \citep{mogstad2024}.
In our pilot on THINGS-EEG2, certificates of this kind, built from simultaneous studentized bounds on pairwise distances, resolved no neighborhood even at 72 trials (Supplementary Section~S1.5), consistent with Table~\ref{tab:snr}.

\subsubsection{Denoised and deeper references}
\citet{lin2024} also judge maps against a denoised signal, obtained by PCA truncation of the same data, to choose hyperparameters for a whole map.
Here the reference comes from held-out repetitions, it is used at the level of individual neighborhoods, and false comfort is measured as a function of the number of trials.
Downsampling studies compare neighbor graphs from shallow data with graphs from deeper data \citep{ahlmann2023}, treating the deeper data as the truth; our extrapolation removes the reference's own noise instead.

\subsubsection{What the estimator adds}
The estimator needs held-out repetitions and gives a dataset-level answer: how much of what a check passes is wrong, with a conservative bound.
It does not certify individual neighborhoods, which the certificates of our pilot could not do at these signal-to-noise ratios either.
It tells an analyst whether reading a map's neighborhoods is warranted at all, and how false comfort falls as trials are added.

\subsection{Limitations}

Several limitations apply.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Scope of the data.} Both datasets are visual responses to static images.
  Other modalities (fMRI, spiking data), other stimulus sets and other response windows may have different ratios of neighbor distance to trial noise, and the effect may be weaker where that ratio is large.
  \item \textbf{One neighborhood definition.} The definition ($k=10$, $\varepsilon=0.2$, $k'=2k$) was varied one factor at a time, not jointly.
  We tested t-SNE, MDS and PCA maps, but not UMAP.
  \item \textbf{The extrapolation assumes a functional form.} We report two extrapolants and judge every claim on the more conservative one.
  The plasmode check found negligible bias, but its truth is itself a finite average of held-out trials, so it checks the estimator under the data's noise rather than against an exact truth.
  \item \textbf{Shared noise counts as signal.} Noise shared by the analyst's and the reference repetitions, such as slow drift within a session, is indistinguishable from signal here.
  Blocks were balanced over sessions (EEG) or over halves of the session (mouse V1) to limit it.
  \item \textbf{Pre-specification.} The EEG confirmation reused the pilot's data with new splits and seeds, and pre-specification was by commits to a version-controlled repository rather than by an external registry \citep{nosek2018}.
  The mouse data are the independent test.
  \item \textbf{Representation.} The representation was noise-normalized.
  Without whitening, false comfort in EEG rose to \EegUnwhitenedFC\% at 8 trials; in mouse V1 it was total either way.
  \item \textbf{Sex and gender.} The EEG participants were reported as 8 female and 2 male, and the mice were \MouseMale{} male and \MouseFemale{} female (Sections~\ref{sec:data-eeg} and~\ref{sec:data-mouse}).
  With 10 subjects per dataset, sex and gender were not analyzed, and neither the human nor the mouse sample can show whether they affect the ratio of neighbor distance to trial noise.
  In mouse V1, false comfort was essentially total in every mouse of either sex (Supplementary Table~S1).
  The EEG sample, ten healthy young adults, is also small and homogeneous in age, which limits how far the human estimates generalize.
  \item \textbf{The comparison of checks} used the primary analysis of each dataset only.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Conclusions}

At the trial budgets common in systems neuroscience, most neighborhoods that look faithful in 2-D maps of trial-averaged responses are not faithful to the responses the averaging estimates.
Checks computed from the same trials do not repair this: permissive checks pass many unfaithful neighborhoods, and strict ones pass almost none.
Local neighborhoods in such maps should therefore be read only against a reference built from held-out repetitions.
The estimator presented here provides one, with a conservative bound, and its code, protocols and results are released with the paper.
