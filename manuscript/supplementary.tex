\section{Supplementary methods}

\subsection{Recovering the session of each EEG repetition}
The THINGS-EEG2 preprocessed test files store the 80 repetitions of each image in one array.
The authors' preprocessing code concatenates the four sessions along the repetition axis (session 1 first) and then reorders the repetitions with \texttt{sklearn.utils.shuffle} applied to $0,\dots,79$ with the random state 20200220.
Replaying that permutation gives the session of every stored repetition, 20 per session.
Blocks were then drawn within session, so that every block and every prefix of the analyst's block is balanced over sessions.
Only the session labels were reconstructed; no data value was changed.

\subsection{Why the extrapolation removes the reference's bias as well as its noise}
Reference labels use plug-in distances between sub-block means.
For two images the squared plug-in distance has expectation $\|\mu_i-\mu_j\|^2+\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_i+\Sigma_j)/R_h$, where $\Sigma_i$ is the trial covariance of image $i$.
In the whitened representation the trace terms are nearly equal across images, so they barely change the ranks, and whatever effect they have vanishes as $R_h\to\infty$, together with the reference's noise.
The extrapolation targets exactly that limit.

\subsection{Why checks computed from the analyst's own trials cannot see the error}
This argument was written into the protocol of the comparison before it was run.
Let the analyst's trials of image $i$ be $x_{it}=\mu_i+e_{it}$, $t=1,\dots,R$, with independent Gaussian noise of a common covariance.
Split the trials into two halves $A$ and $B$ of equal size, and write the averages $c_i=(c^A_i+c^B_i)/2$ and the half-difference $\delta_i=(c^A_i-c^B_i)/2$.
Then
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item the halves are $c^A_i=c_i+\delta_i$ and $c^B_i=c_i-\delta_i$, and $\delta$ is independent of $c$: a noise-only perturbation;
  \item the cross-validated squared distances are $\langle c^A_i-c^A_j,c^B_i-c^B_j\rangle=\|c_i-c_j\|^2-\|\delta_i-\delta_j\|^2$, the plug-in squared distances minus a noise-only term;
  \item a bootstrap resample is $c_i$ plus resampled residuals.
\end{itemize}
The averages carry all the information the trials hold about the $\mu_i$; the residuals add only information about the size of the noise.
Every check of the comparison is therefore a function of the averages, the noise level and randomization.
It can be stricter than the plug-in check, but it cannot tell where the averages themselves are wrong.
The certificates of our earlier pilot, which used the averages and the noise level with family-wise error control, resolved no neighborhood of THINGS-EEG2 even at 72 trials.

\subsection{The scDEED null}
scDEED \citep{xia2024} permutes every gene independently across cells and then applies the pipeline's PCA step to the permuted data.
Our pipeline's dimension-reduction step is the fixed representation fitted on block $F$.
We therefore permuted every measured feature of the analyst's averages (EEG channel-time samples; mouse cells) independently across images and passed the permuted averages through the same representation.
Permuting the 20 coordinates of the representation instead would keep the signal's unequal spread across coordinates.
In the synthetic tests of the code, that null resembled structured data so closely that scDEED called 11\% of points trustworthy, against 74\% with the measured-feature null.
The protocol registers the measured-feature null.

\subsection{The pilot's certificates}
Before the analyses reported here, an exploratory pilot on THINGS-EEG2 (protocol committed at \texttt{5024fe6}) tested per-point certificates.
For each displayed neighborhood, it bounded the reach simultaneously, from studentized bounds on all pairwise distances with family-wise error control.
In semi-synthetic draws with resampled real noise, the certificates made no false statement in 3000 draws.
On the real data they resolved no neighborhood as faithful or distorted at $k'=20$, at 8, 16, 32 or 72 repetitions per image, while the plug-in check called 17.5\% (8 repetitions) to 38.1\% (72) of the points faithful.
The obstacle was the signal-to-noise ratio, not the multiplicity correction (main text, Table~4).
